# Source Hierarchy

## A — Primary authoritative law
Constitutions, statutes, regulations, official gazettes, treaties, official judgments, and official legislative or court publications.

## B — Official institutional material
Parliamentary, governmental, judicial, regulatory, and institutional material.

## C — Recognized legal databases
Established professional or institutional repositories.

## D — Academic
Universities, journals, and scholarly research.

## E — Secondary commentary
Recognized explanatory publications.

## F — GLOBAL LAW explanation
Project-generated explanations, summaries, comparisons, and navigation.

Lower-level material may help discover or explain higher-level material, but must not silently replace higher-level authority.

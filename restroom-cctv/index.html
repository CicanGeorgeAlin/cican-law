CCTV IN
RESTROOMS.

A source-led guide for businesses and organisations considering CCTV in toilets and other facilities where people have a heightened expectation of privacy.

Start here: there is no general permission to film inside toilets.
The Irish Data Protection Commission says organisations should avoid CCTV where people have a high expectation of privacy, such as cubicles. The threshold for CCTV in restrooms more generally remains very high. A security purpose, a camera aimed at an entrance, warning signs or technical safeguards do not by themselves make it lawful.

01 · Ireland

What the law asks

Identifiable CCTV footage is personal data. A private business or other organisation acting as controller must comply with the GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. The DPC's restroom guidance is the essential starting point for this unusually intrusive setting.

High expectation of privacy

Toilet cubicles and areas where intimate activity may occur are especially protected. The DPC says organisations should avoid CCTV in such places. Restroom surveillance more generally faces a very high threshold.

DPC: CCTV in Restrooms

Case-specific assessment

There is no universal checklist, camera angle or template that guarantees legality. The controller must prove necessity and proportionality on the actual facts, assess impacts on every group using the facility and implement effective safeguards.

DPC: CCTV guidance for controllers
Important distinction: this guide explains a legal assessment process. It does not tell a business that it can lawfully install a camera in a toilet. In many situations the correct result of the assessment will be not to install one.
02 · The decision pathway

Before any camera is installed

Work through these stages in order. Keep dated records. Do not install first and try to justify it afterwards.

1

Define a precise, legitimate security purpose

Describe the specific harm to be prevented or investigated. Avoid vague aims such as “general security”. Identify the people, place, times, frequency and seriousness of the documented incidents.

2

Collect reliable evidence

Keep incident logs, complaint records, Garda incident references where available, dates and times, witness reports and records of damage or safety interventions. Minimise personal data and restrict access to sensitive incident files.

3

Test less intrusive alternatives first

Document why measures such as staffed supervision, attendants, patrols, access control, improved lighting, emergency call points, layout changes, staff training and targeted security outside the restroom would or would not address the problem. Record results, not assumptions.

4

Ask whether the restroom camera is genuinely necessary

Consider whether the same purpose can be achieved without recording inside the facility. If an external entrance camera, staff presence or another measure can achieve the purpose with less intrusion, that weighs strongly against internal CCTV.

5

Identify the legal basis and document the balancing test

For a private operator, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interests) may be considered where appropriate. Complete the three-part test: identify the interest, demonstrate necessity, and balance it against the rights and reasonable expectations of people recorded. Do not assume consent is a workable solution.

6

Complete a DPIA before processing

Describe the processing and purpose; assess necessity and proportionality; identify risks to customers, workers, children and other vulnerable people; and specify measures to address those risks. Restroom surveillance should receive a detailed, careful assessment. Consult the DPO where one is appointed.

7

Consult affected people

Plan and document meaningful consultation with workers and users of the facilities, as appropriate and safe. Explain the purpose and proposed safeguards. Do not collect more sensitive information than needed.

8

Assess residual risk and Article 36

If the DPIA indicates high residual risk that cannot be sufficiently mitigated, consult the DPC before processing under Article 36 GDPR. Submit the DPIA and ancillary material and request statutory prior consultation in writing. Article 36 is a consultation process, not a general CCTV licence or blanket approval.

9

Obtain independent legal and technical review

Have an Irish data-protection solicitor and suitably qualified DPO/privacy professional examine the actual proposal, floor plan, camera field of view, mirror reflections, recording configuration, retention and access controls. A paper assessment cannot replace inspection of the real system.

10

Make a documented go / no-go decision

Do not proceed if the intrusion is unnecessary, disproportionate, inadequately mitigated or inconsistent with DPC advice. Record the reasons, conditions, decision-maker and review date. If proceeding is considered defensible, operate only within the assessed and reviewed configuration.

03 · Paperwork

What a compliance file should contain

These are working-document recommendations, not official forms or legal certificates. The DPC does not issue a universal “toilet CCTV permit” form.

Core assessment records

  • Written purpose and scope statement
  • Incident register and supporting evidence
  • Alternatives assessment and results
  • Legitimate Interest Assessment, if relying on Article 6(1)(f)
  • Data Protection Impact Assessment
  • DPO advice and management decision
  • Consultation record and response summary

System and accountability records

  • Floor plan and camera field-of-view diagrams
  • Photographic verification of actual view and reflections
  • Privacy masking and configuration test record
  • Controller / processor contracts and responsibilities
  • Retention schedule and deletion test log
  • Access list, authentication and access audit logs
  • Incident disclosure and data-subject request procedures

Transparency records

  • Prominent notice before a person enters a monitored area
  • Full CCTV privacy notice
  • Controller identity and contact details
  • Purpose and legal basis
  • Recipients, security, retention and rights information
  • How to contact the DPC and make a complaint

Regulatory and review records

  • Article 36 submission and complete DPC correspondence, if applicable
  • Record of advice, conditions and actions taken
  • Independent legal opinion
  • Installer licence verification
  • Periodic DPIA and necessity review
  • Change-control record for any camera movement or software change
Do not publish sensitive records. Garda references, incident reports, floor plans, camera positions, security configurations and DPC correspondence should remain in the controller's restricted internal file. Do not upload them to this public website.
04 · Technical safeguards

If a proposal survives the legal assessment

Safeguards can reduce risk; they cannot cure an unjustified location or make disproportionate surveillance lawful.

06 · European Union

One GDPR framework. National rules still matter.

The GDPR applies across the EU, and the European Data Protection Board's Guidelines 3/2019 address processing through video devices. However, national laws, supervisory authority procedures, employment rules, security licensing and local legal interpretation can differ. An Irish assessment is not a permit for another Member State.

EU-wide starting points

  • EDPB Guidelines 3/2019
  • GDPR — Regulation (EU) 2016/679
  • EDPB members and national supervisory authorities

Country-by-country research required

Before applying this guide outside Ireland, identify the relevant national supervisory authority, national data-protection legislation, employment consultation duties, surveillance rules, installer licensing and any sector-specific restrictions.

# GLOBAL LAW — CITIZEN-FIRST PRODUCT SPECIFICATION

## Purpose

GLOBAL LAW must be usable by a person who knows nothing about legal terminology.

The citizen must never be required to know the name of an Act, court, legal doctrine or legal category before beginning.

## Primary journeys

### 1. I HAVE A QUESTION
Question → jurisdiction → situation → legal area → concepts → sources → provisions → explanation → evidence.

### 2. I HAVE A LEGAL PROBLEM
Problem → minimum necessary context → possible legal areas → relevant rules → official authorities/sources.

This is educational navigation, not individualized legal representation.

### 3. EXPLAIN THIS LAW
Original provision → simple explanation → who it affects → conditions → exceptions → rights/duties → consequences → related provisions → source.

### 4. CHECK THIS CLAIM
Claim → jurisdiction/date clarification → evidence search → source comparison → result:
- SUPPORTED
- PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
- NOT VERIFIED
- CONTRADICTED BY SOURCE
- SOURCE UNAVAILABLE
- JURISDICTION/DATE UNCLEAR

### 5. WHAT CHANGED?
Previous version → new version → effective date → amendment/repeal event → source → explanation.

### 6. COMPARE COUNTRIES
Concept/problem → jurisdictions → comparable provisions → comparison class → evidence → limitations.

## Human-readable legal vocabulary

The interface should translate legal structure into citizen language:

- Right — what the law allows/protects/entitles a person to, where supported.
- Duty — what the law requires.
- Power — what an institution or person is legally empowered to do.
- Prohibition — what the law forbids.
- Condition — what must be true for a rule to apply.
- Exception — when a general rule does not apply.
- Remedy — what legal response may be available.
- Penalty — a legally prescribed consequence where applicable.

These labels are explanatory classifications and must not silently change the meaning of the underlying provision.

## Evidence-first presentation

Every substantive explanation should make it easy to reach:

1. original provision;
2. authoritative source;
3. version/date;
4. court interpretation where relevant;
5. provenance;
6. verification status.

## Safety boundaries

GLOBAL LAW must:
- distinguish information from individualized legal advice;
- avoid unsupported conclusions;
- display uncertainty;
- never invent citations;
- never imply that a translation is authoritative unless its authority is established;
- never present an AI explanation as the law itself.

## Accessibility

Citizen mode should support:
- mobile-first navigation;
- keyboard navigation;
- screen readers;
- adjustable text;
- high contrast;
- simple-language mode;
- multilingual navigation;
- accessible tables;
- clear source links.

## Success criterion

A person without legal training should be able to start with a normal-language question and reach the authoritative legal source without first learning the legal system's vocabulary.

# Evidence and Provenance Standard

## Rule
NO PROVENANCE, NO VERIFIED CLAIM.

Every important legal object should identify jurisdiction, object type, official title, source authority, source URL or persistent identifier, original language, precise locator where available, relevant legal dates, retrieval and verification date, evidence level, research state, and known limitations.

## Evidence states
VERIFIED — adequate authoritative evidence supports the claim.
PARTIALLY VERIFIED — important elements are verified but meaningful gaps remain.
REQUIRES INVESTIGATION — discovered or plausible but not yet verified.
SOURCE UNAVAILABLE — relevant authority could not be reliably accessed.

## Source failure
A broken URL does not automatically mean a law disappeared. Record the last successful retrieval, source identity, authority, failure date, alternative official source if found, and recovery status.

## Contradictions
Never silently choose between conflicting sources. Investigate source hierarchy, dates, versions, jurisdiction, amendments, and translation differences.

## AI provenance
An AI explanation inherits the limitations of its evidence. If the evidence is uncertain, the explanation cannot be labelled certain.

## Auditability
A future researcher must be able to reconstruct why a legal statement was included and what evidence supported it.

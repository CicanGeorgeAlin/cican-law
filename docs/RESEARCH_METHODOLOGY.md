# GLOBAL LAW Research Methodology

## Standard investigation sequence

1. Define jurisdiction and scope.
2. Identify the official constitutional source.
3. Identify official legislative sources and gazettes.
4. Identify official court sources.
5. Identify government and institutional sources.
6. Identify regional and supranational sources.
7. Identify international obligations.
8. Establish the legal hierarchy.
9. Identify terminology and original-language terms.
10. Map major legal subjects.
11. Record relationships between legal objects.
12. Verify legal status and temporal information.
13. Cross-check material claims.
14. Assign an Investigation Gate level.
15. Produce citizen-facing explanations only after evidence review.

## Research discipline

Do not begin with a conclusion and search for supporting material.

Start with the legal question, collect authoritative evidence, identify conflicts and gaps, then explain what the evidence supports.

## Currentness

A source discovered today is not automatically evidence that a law is currently in force. Currentness requires temporal verification.

## Completeness

No jurisdiction can be declared complete simply because a large number of documents have been collected. Coverage must be measured against the jurisdiction's legal architecture.

## Uncertainty

Uncertainty is data. Record it explicitly and preserve the question for later investigation.

## Reproducibility

Another researcher should be able to follow the recorded sources and understand how the project reached its conclusion.

# GLOBAL LAW — SOURCE ACQUISITION AND ARCHIVAL POLICY

## Purpose

GLOBAL LAW is source-first. Legal knowledge must be traceable to authoritative material whenever such material is available.

## Source hierarchy

### Tier A — Primary authoritative law
- constitutions
- statutes/Acts
- codes
- regulations
- official gazettes
- treaties
- official court judgments
- official legislative publications
- authoritative official legal databases

### Tier B — Official institutional material
- ministries
- courts
- legislatures
- regulators
- official explanatory material
- official procedural guidance

### Tier C — Recognized legal databases
Used as discovery, verification or structured access where appropriate. Primary sources remain preferred.

### Tier D — Academic sources
Useful for history, doctrine, context and analysis.

### Tier E — Secondary commentary
Useful for orientation and interpretation, never silently upgraded to primary authority.

### Tier F — GLOBAL LAW explanations
Plain-language educational material produced by the project. Never presented as law.

## Acquisition rules

For every source, record where possible:
- authority;
- title;
- identifier;
- official URL;
- language;
- publication date;
- effective date;
- retrieval date;
- version;
- source type;
- legal status;
- accessibility;
- licence/access conditions;
- archival reference;
- verification history.

## Copyright and access

GLOBAL LAW must distinguish:
- public-domain material;
- government/public legal material;
- openly licensed material;
- material that may be linked but not reproduced;
- material requiring permission;
- material whose reuse status is unclear.

Do not assume that because a legal document is publicly accessible online, every form of reproduction is unrestricted.

Where full reproduction is not legally appropriate, preserve precise citation and link to the authoritative source.

## Source failure

If an official source is unavailable:
1. record the failure;
2. identify alternative authoritative or recognized sources;
3. preserve the uncertainty;
4. do not silently substitute a secondary source as primary authority;
5. schedule re-verification where appropriate.

## Link resilience

A URL is not a permanent identity.

Every source must receive a stable internal source ID. Where legally permissible and technically appropriate, retain archival metadata, retrieval information and integrity information so that source history can be reconstructed.

## Source change

When a source changes:
- preserve the previous source record;
- identify the new version;
- record the change event;
- update legal-object relationships;
- re-run applicable Investigation Gates;
- do not overwrite history.

## Verification

A source becomes VERIFIED only after the applicable evidence and authority checks have passed.

Source discovery is not source verification.

## Principle

**NO PROVENANCE, NO VERIFIED CLAIM.**
